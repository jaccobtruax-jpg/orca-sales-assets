01 · WHAT WE HEARD

Three things you said, three things we do

You were clear on the call: reviews need to come back faster, they can't stall on provider hiccups, and you don't want to keep planning around account-level throughput caps. Here is each one, and the OrcaRouter mechanism that answers it.

Reliability

Reviews that stall on provider errors

You've built retry handling into your product because a single provider error can stall a session for minutes, or drop a review outright.

Fix: fallback chains. A request that gets a 429 or 5xx from one provider is served by the next in the chain, automatically. No retry UI for your customers to watch.

Speed

Review latency

You shipped a Flash review variant to cut latency, which means you're maintaining a special low-latency code path in your pipeline.

Fix: per-request routing. Easy diffs go to a small, fast model; hard ones escalate to frontier. Flash becomes a routing policy, not a separate code path.

Throughput

Provider account ceilings

You're on direct Anthropic and OpenAI accounts, so your burst ceiling is their rate limit tier. Ten PRs landing Monday morning means ten concurrent review fleets hitting one meter.

Fix: no per-model rate limits. Bursts spread across every provider we carry instead of dying on one account cap. The only throttle is a workspace-level bucket, which we can size to your traffic.

02 · SETUP

Running in one afternoon

  1. Create a workspace at orcarouter.ai/console and generate an API key. Two minutes.
  2. Decide on keys. Either use platform routing, or attach your existing Anthropic and OpenAI keys under BYOK at orcarouter.ai/console/byok. BYOK keeps your current contracts and invoices; you pay a small platform fee instead of token markup. Your choice, and you can mix both per key.
  3. Add a fallback chain to your scanner calls. The code sample below is a drop-in: you keep your primary model, and the chain takes over if it fails.
  4. Try orcarouter/auto on the Flash path. It sizes each request to how hard it is, and learns from your traffic. If you need deterministic behavior anywhere, keep the model pinned there. You choose per call.
One honest caveat before you see it live: we don't expose per-model rate limits, but a workspace-level bucket does exist. It's there to keep the platform predictable, and we size it to your expected volume up front, so it never surprises you mid-burst.
03 · CODE

What the calls look like

Fallback chain, curl

Your primary model stays first. On a 429, 5xx, or network error, the chain serves the request from the next model. Billing lands on whichever model actually served.

# One call. Up to 5 models in the chain.
curl https://api.orcarouter.ai/v1/chat/completions \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $ORCA_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -H "X-OrcaRouter-Include-Cost: true" \
  -d '{
    "model": "openai/gpt-4o",
    "extra_body": {
      "models": [
        "openai/gpt-4o",
        "anthropic/claude-haiku-4.5",
        "google/gemini-2.5-pro"
      ]
    },
    "messages": [
      {"role": "user", "content": "Review this diff for correctness and security."}
    ]
  }'

Response headers tell you what happened: X-Orca-Fallback-Model shows which model served the request, X-Orca-Request-Id is your lookup key for cost and logs, and with the cost header on, usage.cost_usd carries the settled price of that call.

Same thing from Go

Standard OpenAI client, base URL swapped, chain passed as an extra body field.

// go get github.com/openai/openai-go  (or your existing SDK)
client := openai.NewClient(openai.ClientConfig{
    BaseURL: "https://api.orcarouter.ai/v1",
    APIKey:  os.Getenv("ORCA_KEY"),
})

resp, err := client.Chat.Completions.New(ctx, openai.ChatCompletionNewParams{
    Model: "openai/gpt-4o",
    Messages: []openai.ChatCompletionMessageParamUnion{
        openai.UserMessage("Review this diff."),
    },
    ExtraBody: map[string]any{
        "models": []string{
            "openai/gpt-4o",
            "anthropic/claude-haiku-4.5",
            "google/gemini-2.5-pro",
        },
    },
})

Auto router for the Flash path

Same endpoint, orcarouter/auto as the model. It weighs quality, cost, latency, and reliability per request. The X-Orca-Resolved-Model response header tells you what it picked.

curl https://api.orcarouter.ai/v1/chat/completions \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $ORCA_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "model": "orcarouter/auto",
    "messages": [
      {"role": "user", "content": "Quick pass: obvious bugs or merge-blocking issues only."}
    ]
  }'
04 · YOUR STACK

Why this fits how Tenki actually runs

  • You're on two providers today. Your pipeline calls Anthropic and OpenAI directly. With OrcaRouter, a provider outage or 429 no longer needs a code path in your product. The chain handles it, and your customers never see a "retrying" message in their PR again.
  • Your reviews are a pipeline, not one call. Scanners in parallel, a verification step, model overrides you A/B test. Every one of those calls can carry its own chain or routing policy. Nothing about the architecture changes; the endpoint and the fallback do.
  • The dollar per review is the margin. You charge $1.00 per review. Routing the easy scans to small, cheap models while keeping 0% token markup drops your cost of goods on every review, not just the hard ones.
  • Your own benchmark says quality is your weapon. 68.9% recall on the 2026 benchmark you published is a strong number. You should keep the frontier models on the hard reviews, and we give you a way to do that without paying frontier prices on the easy ones.
  • Zero data retention by default. Customer code is the whole product. We keep no data by default, and if your workspace holds a ZDR grant it applies to BYOK keys too. Your data-handling story stays intact across every model in the catalog, not just the two providers you've signed agreements with.
Determinism is yours. If you need a pinned model for verification or for benchmark consistency, pin it. Per-request routing is a choice, not a requirement. You'll never see a model you didn't approve appear in a chain you didn't write.

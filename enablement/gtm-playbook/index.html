Booking Meetings from Socialhow-to

LinkedIn · X · Reddit — find the right people, get their attention, book the meeting. October 2026.

The whole system in one paragraph: you don't send cold messages to strangers. You get visible first — comments, replies, posts — and then you message people who have already seen you two or three times. The message is the harvest, not the planting. That one change is the difference between a 1–3% reply rate and a 10–20% one.
Why this works (the numbers behind it)
  • Cold DMs reply at 1–3%. Messages to people who have never seen you get deleted in the two-second scan.
  • Warm DMs reply at 10–20%. Same message, same person — but they've seen your comment on their post, or your reply in a thread. 5–10x difference, made entirely in public before the DM exists.
  • Personalization is 2–3x. A message with their name, their company, and one true fact about them beats a template by 2–3x on replies.
  • 70% of senders quit after the first touch. Replies concentrate on touches 2 and 3. Most senders never see the replies their message earned — persistence alone is an edge.
  • Cold email is capped. Deliverability rules now limit cold email to ~30–50/day per mailbox. Social is where the volume and the meetings are.
How a meeting actually happens — the seven steps
1
Find a person with a signal.

A signal is a reason to reach out: they changed jobs, their company raised money, they're hiring AI people, they posted about LLM costs or OpenRouter, they complained about provider fees. (The full signal list is in the LinkedIn tab.)

2
Comment on their post.

A genuine thought that adds to what they said. One to three sentences. Never "Great post!" — that's noise, and it teaches them to ignore you. (Examples in the LinkedIn tab.)

3
Engage again within a week.

Another comment on their next post, or a sharp reply on a thread they're in. Now you've been visible twice.

4
Send the warm DM.

Reference both touches, ask one question about their stack, keep it under 90 words. No pitch. (Template C in the templates tab.)

5
They reply.

Stay conversational. One exchange of substance. Don't pitch in message two.

6
Make the small ask.

The benchmarked CTA: "Would you have a couple minutes to chat about this over the next few days?" Small, specific, easy to say yes to.

7
The meeting.

Log it. Note which signal and which message produced it — that's the data that tells you what to do more of.

The one number that matters

Warm DM reply rate. Track it per template: anything under 2% gets killed, anything over 8% gets scaled. The category average for cold is 3.4%; the best senders run 10.7%. You're aiming at the top decile — that's one meeting per 30–50 prospects touched.

Step 1 — Make your profile a storefront

Here's what happens when your warm DM lands: the person clicks your name and looks at your profile. If it's empty or generic, the warm DM dies right there. If it shows a real person who works on their problem, the DM lands better than anything you wrote.

  • Photo. A real, clear headshot. Non-negotiable.
  • Headline that says what you do and who for, not your title: "Help teams spending $10K+/mo on AI cut inference cost — OrcaRouter" beats "Account Executive @ FlashLabs".
  • Featured post: pin one post that shows a point of view — something like "What 14B tokens through OpenRouter actually costs, fees included." It proves you know the space.
  • About: three sentences. Who you help, what you help with, one proof point.

Step 3 — The comment (the part everyone gets wrong)

A comment is a DM with witnesses. It's how you become visible before you ever message someone. Three rules:

  • Add a thought. Build on what they said, or gently push back, or ask a real question about their post. One to three sentences.
  • Never "Great post!" It's noise. It teaches them to scroll past your name.
  • No product. The comment is not the pitch. The DM is the pitch — and it only works because the comment wasn't.

Why all three? Because 70% of senders quit after touch one, and replies concentrate on touches two and three. The people who reply to your DM are often replying to your persistence, not your words.

Step 5 — When they reply
  • Answer their question fully, with specifics. This is where the competence shows — one sharp technical point beats three value props.
  • Keep it to one substantive exchange before the ask. Don't pitch in message two; earn message three.
  • Then the small ask: "Would you have a couple minutes to chat about this over the next few days?"
  • If they go quiet after replying, send one value-add follow-up in 3–5 days — a data point or observation about their stack, never "just following up."
Read this first: tech people are slow to reply to DMs on X and Reddit — that's not a bug, it's the nature of the channels. They're public squares, not inboxes. So X and Reddit are where you build credibility and find pain, not where you send DMs. The meetings come from LinkedIn; X and Reddit feed it the names and the context.
X — the signal channel

What to do daily (20 minutes):

  1. Run the pain searches. These are live lists of people who announced their problem publicly:
    • "OpenRouter fees"
    • "OpenRouter markup"
    • "switching from OpenRouter"
    • "LLM costs out of control"
    • "we built our own router" or "routing LLMs"
    Each hit is a prospect with a documented reason to care. Note the name, their stack, and what they said — that's your LinkedIn signal list for later.
  2. Reply publicly, once. Find one post in the LLM/routing discourse where you can add a sharp technical point. A public reply is a DM with witnesses — it builds the account that makes future DMs land.
  3. Quote-post with a take. Not agreement — a take. ("Everyone's chasing cheaper models. The fee structure is where the money actually leaks.") This is how the account becomes a person with a point of view.

When a DM on X is OK:

  • They replied to you publicly first.
  • They asked you something.
  • They posted a pain signal and you can open with their exact words. (Template D works here.)

Never: cold DMs to people you've never engaged with, pitch-slapping under their posts, or automating "great thread" replies. That's how accounts get muted and brands get blocked.

Reddit — the credibility channel

Where to be:

  • r/LLMDevs, r/LocalLLaMA, r/OpenAI, r/ArtificialIntelligence

What to do weekly (2–3 genuine answers):

  1. Answer questions with real technical value. People ask how to pick between models, how to cut API costs, whether to build their own router. Answer properly, with specifics. No links until someone asks.
  2. Monitor the pain threads. "OpenRouter charging me 5.5%", "our LLM bill doubled", "should we build a router?" — each is a warm lead with a documented problem. Save the name and context to your signal list.
  3. DM only after you helped them publicly. "I saw your thread about routing costs — happy to share how we think about it." After a public thread where you were useful, this is welcome. Before that, it's spam.

Never: cold DMs, dropping links in answers, or answering with "we built a product for that." Reddit's audience smells a pitch instantly and will torch the account's credibility.

How X and Reddit feed LinkedIn

The workflow: X and Reddit surface the pain and the names → you add those people to your LinkedIn signal list with their context (what they said, their stack) → the LinkedIn three-touch sequence runs with a ready-made, specific opener. The channels aren't separate systems; they're one funnel with three intake points.

Every template is under 90 words, has one question, and ends with a small ask. The {{fields}} are where the research goes — if you can't fill them, you haven't found the right person yet.

What to post (the one piece a week)

A POV post is one sharp idea about the space, with no ask attached. It exists so your profile proves you know what you're talking about when people click it. Examples that work:

  • "What 14B tokens through OpenRouter actually costs — including the fees they don't advertise."
  • "Everyone's chasing cheaper models. The fee structure is where the money actually leaks."
  • "We keep seeing teams build their own router. Here's what it really costs to maintain one."

One idea, your own words, no CTA. Reps can comment on each other's posts to seed engagement — a post with three real comments looks like a conversation, because it is one.

Tracking — keep it boring and simple

A spreadsheet with one row per prospect: name · company · signal (why you reached out) · touch 1 date · touch 2 date · DM date · template used · reply? · meeting? · notes. Friday's review is just a pivot on the template column: reply rate per template, kill or scale. That's the whole system.

The 2-second test: a technical buyer decides to read or delete in under two seconds. Buzzwords, "curious if you're looking…", blocks of text, fear + exclamation = delete. Their name, their number, their models, one question = read.

The straight line to a smaller bill

A working session on the method behind the OrcaRouter pitch: straight line selling, discovery that earns the demo, listening that finds the real pain, and a demo flow built to prove, not perform.

Deck: Enterprise Briefing (12 slides, confidential) Intel: live sites + RouterArena board, 2026-08-26 Audience: sales, AEs, founding team
Build: v1.0 2026-08-26
Claim hygiene, first
Every figure on this page is vendor-reported unless it carries a live source and date. The 30-day replay pilot is the defense against every skepticism: "That is exactly what the pilot measures, on your traffic, before you commit anything." The Enterprise commitment is a floor on spend routed through the platform, never a fee. Core line, learn it cold: "the only thing we have to sell you is a smaller bill."

00The method: straight line selling

Jordan Belfort's straight line is the shortest path between the opening and the close: you sell on a line, and the line is only as straight as your discovery. Every detour (a feature dump, a rambling story, a pitch before you know the pain) bends the line. The OrcaRouter demo is a line with six stops. This section gives you the skeleton; the demo flow section builds the flesh.

4Stops on the line: open, discover, present, close
3Tonality levers: rhythm, tempo, clarity
3Belief stack: yourself, the product, the company
11-14Questions top reps ask in a 30-min discovery (Gong-reported)

The line, mapped to an OrcaRouter call

  1. Pre-call: pick your signalOne researched fact about the account: a changelog entry about retries, a job post for agent infra, a public outage post, a cost line. This is your opening line, not small talk.
  2. Open (0-2 min)Name the signal, state the agenda, ask permission to diagnose. "I saw your changelog note about re-prompting on provider errors. Is it fair to say reliability is costing your team time today?"
  3. Discover (2-10 min)Closed questions that isolate ONE pillar of pain: cost, reliability, security, or lock-in. Loop after every 2-3 answers. Do not pitch yet.
  4. Agree the target (10 min)Loop the pain back in their words, then ask the permission question: "If I can show you how that works in your stack, is that worth the next 15 minutes?"
  5. Present (10-22 min)Demo ONE pillar. Every feature gets a benefit with "which means." Every claim gets proof: live router watch, a request log, the pricing table $0.00 column.
  6. Prove and close (22-30 min)The replay pilot: their logs, offline, measured deltas, threshold set by them. Then close on the next step, not the contract. The contract is a floor, and it comes later.

Tonality: how you say it

Belfort's second pillar. Rhythm (don't machine-gun), tempo (slow down on the money line), tonality (fall at the end of statements, rise on questions), clarity (no filler). Two OrcaRouter applications:

  • The money line is said slowly, with a full stop after it: "The only thing we have to sell you is a smaller bill." Let it sit.
  • Questions fall at the end. Statements rise. If you hear yourself rising at the end of "we can measure that on your logs," you are asking permission for a fact. Fix it.

Belief: the third pillar

Belief in yourself (you know the method), the product (you have used it), the company (you have read the paper). Belief is not cheerleading. It is the absence of hedging. You never say "we think" about a measured claim; you say "we measured." You never say "hopefully" before the pilot; you say "here is the threshold, you decide it."

The ruleIf you can't prove it in the demo, don't claim it in the discovery. If you can prove it, claim it flat.

The pattern under all of them
Every commercial rival sells the same promise: frontier quality at a fraction of the price. NotDiamond says it, Weave says it, Nadir says it, LiteLLM implies it. 03Discovery: questions that earn the demo

Discovery is where the straight line gets straight. The goal is not to fill a template, it is to isolate ONE pillar of pain the prospect owns, in their words, with a number attached. Ask closed questions to eliminate, open questions to open up, loop every 2-3 answers, and never pitch until you can say the pain back to them and they agree.

Universal openers (any persona)

01Spend

"What is the biggest line item in your AI budget today?"

02Decision

"How do you decide which model a request goes to?"

03Failover

"What happens when your primary provider degrades?"

04Adoption

"How many teams are shipping on models right now?"

05Governance

"How does a new model get approved around here?"

Question banks by persona

CFO / FinOps persona

  • What is your monthly inference spend across providers?
  • How is that split between OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google today?
  • How many engineers or seats are generating that spend?
  • Of that traffic, how much do you think actually needs the most expensive model?
  • How do you attribute cost back to teams today, or do you?
  • Is that spend forecasted and budgeted, or is it running hot?
  • What would a 30-40% reduction on that line fund instead?
  • Who owns the AI budget, and when does the next budget cycle close?

Platform / Engineering persona

  • How many model endpoints do you integrate with today?
  • What does your failover look like: code, config, or hope?
  • Last time a provider degraded, how long until you noticed, and what did it cost?
  • When a new frontier model ships, who re-benchmarks it against your workloads?
  • Can you pin a workload to a specific model today, or is that a code change?
  • What is the deploy cost of switching a model for one of your products?
  • Have you hit throughput or rate limits that capped your product?
  • How much engineering time goes to gateway plumbing per month?

CISO / Security persona

  • How are models approved before a team can use them?
  • Do teams adopt models faster than security reviews them? (asked gently)
  • What is your data retention posture with each provider today?
  • Where does your data flow when it hits a model, and can you prove it?
  • How do you audit which model touched which request, if at all?
  • Are secrets or PII showing up in prompts today, and how do you know?
  • What would enforced, logged-with-evidence governance change for you?

The ladder: agree, value, commit

  1. Agree"So the pain is: most of your traffic pays the frontier rate, and nobody has measured the split. Did I get that right?"
  2. Value"If a replay of your own logs showed you a 30-40% cut at a quality delta you set, what is that worth to you?" Let them answer in dollars or priority.
  3. Commit"If I can show you how that runs in your stack in the next 15 minutes, is that worth your time now?" This is the only question you ask before the demo.

Deal structure notes (from the playbook)

The 15-minute discovery call skeleton: 0-2 open with the signal, 2-5 diagnose, 5-10 show ONE pillar, 10-13 close, 13-15 next step. The typical SLG deal is a minimum monthly committed spend: a floor on spend routed through the platform, never a fee. Example shape (illustrative): a $10k/mo prospect commits $5k/mo, saves 30-40% net, and the floor is how the saving gets shared. Never describe the commitment as a fee; a fee against spend eats the entire savings math.

04Active listening: seek first to understand

The reference is Stephen Covey's The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People (it is seven, not twelve). Habit 5 is "Seek First to Understand, Then to Be Understood." Covey's line: "Most people do not listen with the intent to understand; they listen with the intent to reply." In a demo, that is the difference between pitching at the prospect and pitching into the prospect.

The five levels of listening (Covey)

  1. IgnoringNot listening. Reading your notes, checking the clock.
  2. Pretending"Yeah, uh-huh, mhm" while thinking about your next slide.
  3. SelectiveHearing only the parts that match your pitch.
  4. AttentivePaying attention to the words, but listening from your own frame.
  5. EmpathicListening inside the other person's frame of reference. This is the level that sells. "Diagnose before you prescribe": a doctor who prescribes before the diagnosis is malpractice, and a demo before the diagnosis is bad selling.

The mechanics that make it visible

LOOPListen, absorb, confirm

The Gong-taught skill top reps use: after they answer, say their pain back in your words. "So what you're telling me is that every provider failure today means a re-prompt and a user waiting. Is that right?" Then stop.

MIRRORLast three words

Echo their final phrase as a question: Prospect: "We just default everything to Opus." You: "Default everything to Opus?" They expand. Cheap, effective, slightly disarming.

LABELName the feeling

"That sounds like it's been expensive in more ways than money." Naming the emotion earns trust and often produces the real objection underneath.

SILENCEThree beats

When they finish, count three beats before you speak. They will fill it with the truth. Also: never interrupt a prospect. It is the single fastest way to bend the line.

STACKSQuestions in threes

Ask three questions on one topic, then loop. Stacks of three keep discovery structured instead of wandering, which is what top discovery calls do.

NOTESWrite it down

Physically write their numbers and phrases. It signals that what they said matters, and it gives you the exact words to loop back later.

Ratios to hold

  • Talk ratio in discovery: roughly 45% you, 55% them (Gong-reported for top discovery calls). If you are over 50%, you are pitching, not diagnosing.
  • 11-14 questions in a 30-minute discovery call (Gong-reported). If you have asked fewer than 6 by minute 10, you are behind.
  • One question at a time. A two-part question gets a one-part answer and a confused prospect.

Drill for the session

Pairs, 8 minutes each. Rep A has a hidden pain card (examples: "my team defaults everything to the most expensive model", "we got burned by a provider outage last quarter", "security won't approve any proxy"). Rep B must run discovery without pitching: ask closed questions, stack in threes, loop back the pain in their own words, and only then offer the demo line. The pair debriefs: did B hear the pain before presenting? Which loop landed? Which question did A dodge, and why?

05The demo flow: act by act

Thirty minutes, six acts, one pillar. The demo is the present step of the straight line: it should feel like the discovery continuing, not a scripted tour. Every feature you show was selected because this prospect's discovery said it matters. If discovery pointed at cost, you do not show the firewall. If it pointed at governance, you do not show the cost chart.

Act 0: pre-call (before the meeting)

  • Pull the account signal: changelog, job post, outage, spend story, their stack.
  • Decide the ONE pillar hypothesis: cost, reliability, security, or anti-lock-in.
  • Prepare their-numbers examples: if they told us spend and providers, use them.
  • Verify the claims you plan to quote against the ledger below.

Act 5: close (28-30)

  • Loop the pain one last time, confirm the pillar.
  • Offer the pilot: W1 replay offline, W2-3 shadow on their keys, W4 canary or walk.
  • Set the next step: log export, trial workspace, security review in parallel.
  • If a deal shapes, name the shape: committed monthly floor, never a fee.

Enterprise variant (45-60 min)

  • Two pillars max, one per decision-maker: CFO gets cost, platform gets reliability, CISO gets governance. Invite all three.
  • Add the security questionnaire walkthrough (deck Appendix B, 13 shipped controls): SAML/OIDC, RBAC, audit logs, allow-lists, residency, ZDR, self-hosted Docker partials. Every status real; never let diligence be the first time they hear a gap.
  • Add deployment options: managed SaaS with region select, private deployment, self-hosted, OSS Lite.
  • Close on the pilot with the security review running in parallel, not after.
Demo traps
Never demo more than one pillar. Never say "no limits" (a workspace bucket exists). Never quote a model name you have not verified against the docs. Never present 62/27/11 as their split; it is illustrative, and the pilot measures theirs. Never say $0 fees on BYOK or Enterprise without the pricing-page qualifiers.

Claim traps
  • "0% BYOK fees": docs default is a 5% platform fee, configurable. Quote the pricing page, never a number from memory.
  • "No fees at Enterprise": the pricing FAQ says Enterprise may include a flat per-million-token routing fee depending on volume tier.
  • "#2 on RouterArena" or any rank claim: we are #12 of 31 as of 2026-08-26. Always quote accuracy and cost with the snapshot date, never a rank.
  • "$25 signup credit": does not exist on the live site.
  • "$499 Team plan": internal repos only; live page says $49.
  • "No limits at all": a workspace rate bucket exists. The pitch is "no per-model ceilings," never "no limits."

08Teach-back: what changed, what we learned

Research findings for this session, in plain language, with the decisions they imply.

What changed in the market since our last intel

  • Weave Router now scores above us on the live board (arena 72.82 vs 72.08, acc 76.32 vs 75.54, cost $0.94 vs $1.00, 8/26 snapshot). Decision needed: how reps answer "Weave is ranked higher." Recommended answer is in the Q&A playbook: don't fight the board, fight the method and the deal, and let the replay be the measurement.
  • Nadir ships three board entries (Tumbler #5, Caliper #7, Router #11) and its live pricing is savings-share: 25% of the first $2k, 10% above. Their "60% cheaper, 98% of always-Opus quality" is a modeled claim; our replay measures.
  • Martian is off the live board (was 61.6 in the deck). Unknown whether commercial or abandoned. Flag for verification.
  • Portkey rebranded to Prisma Airs AI Gateway and is running an enterprise webinar motion. Suite sprawl is the beatable part.
  • OpenRouter is adding routing and guardrail features (auto routing, guardrails, ZDR attribution, zero completion insurance) and its auto-routing is powered by NotDiamond. Feature state rots fast; re-verify any "they lack X" claim before quoting.
  • Our own pricing page FAQ now admits Enterprise may include a flat per-million-token routing fee. Quote zero-markup for Hacker/Team and let the pricing page speak for Enterprise.

What we learned about the craft

  • The reference the team asked for is Covey's 7 Habits (not 12), and the listening habit is Habit 5: Seek First to Understand, Then to Be Understood. The empathic listening level is the one that sells, and it is a drillable skill: loop, mirror, label, silence, stacks of three.
  • Cost-plus-quality is table stakes across every competitor. The wedge is structural: incentive (zero markup), proof (published method + replay pilot), and enforcement (governance that blocks instead of logs).
  • The straight line and the demo flow are the same shape: open on a signal, diagnose to one pillar, agree the target, present one pillar with proof, prove with the pilot, close on the next step.
  • The floor-not-fee framing is the deal-shape linchpin. "The only thing we have to sell you is a smaller bill" is the line every rep should be able to say cold.

Quick win / loss guide

vs OpenRouter, you win when

  • The buyer cares about total cost, not per-token list price (5.5% + 5% fees compound)
  • FinOps or the CFO has a seat at the table
  • They want learned routing, not a model picker
  • Governance is on the checklist (SOC 2, HIPAA, 32 packs)
  • They run >1M BYOK requests/mo (the 5% bracket)

vs OpenRouter, you lose when

  • A solo developer just wants credits, fast
  • Community and marketplace size decide the deal
  • They already have credits sunk in OpenRouter
  • Procurement mandates the incumbent

vs Martian / NotDiamond, you win when

  • Benchmark evidence matters (75.5 vs 61.6 / 60.8)
  • They need general workloads, not just coding agents
  • Zero margin and zero BYOK fees are the requirement
  • Compliance has to be built in, not bolted on

vs Martian / NotDiamond, you lose when

  • They're already embedded in a harness NotDiamond is tuned for
  • Their workload is narrow and the competitor's router is purpose-built for it
  • Entry price matters: $0.05/M and 2,500 free requests are cheap to trial

vs LiteLLM, you win when

  • They don't want to operate a gateway, and have no headcount for re-benchmarking
  • They want learned routing, not YAML rules
  • They want attested compliance without running it themselves
  • Their model catalogue moves faster than their ops team can track

vs LiteLLM, you lose when

  • Air-gapped or fully on-prem is a hard requirement they must own
  • A strong platform team wants to run it, and it's free
  • Data residency demands strict self-hosting only

Objections you'll hear, and answers

"We'll just run LiteLLM ourselves, it's free"

Free license, not free operation. Someone owns the gateway: upgrades, incidents, and re-benchmarking a model catalogue that changes weekly. LiteLLM routes on static rules you write; when prices shift, your rules drift until a human notices.

Our point: try the replay. We route your logged traffic offline for a week and show you the delta. If the numbers don't convince you, you keep the measurement and we part ways. No ops burden assumed.

"OpenRouter has more models and no per-token markup either"

True on both counts: the catalogue is big and per-token prices are passthrough. The margin lives elsewhere. A 5.5% platform fee on credit purchases with a $0.80 minimum, so small top-ups pay 10-20%. And a 5% BYOK fee once you exceed 1M requests a month. At $10k/mo of spend, that's $550+/mo you don't see on any token chart.

We charge $0 on tokens and $0 on BYOK. And we route for you: 75.5% on RouterArena, which beats GPT-5, and 99% with escalation. They sell a catalogue. We sell a decision engine.

"We don't trust vendor benchmark claims"

Correct, you shouldn't. That's why the pilot starts with an offline replay of your own traffic, measured against your quality bar, on your logs. Week 1: replay. Weeks 2-3: shadow. Week 4: canary. The delta is your number, not ours. Miss the agreed threshold and you walk, keeping the measurement.

"We're locked into our OpenAI/Anthropic agreements"

Fine. Bring your own keys. We route over your existing provider contracts; your negotiated rates and data terms stay exactly as they are, and we charge 0% BYOK fees. What you gain is routing that sends each prompt to the cheapest model that clears your bar, plus session-pinned caching so you keep provider cache discounts. Your commitments still draw down. Your bill gets smaller.

"Security and compliance are dealbreakers"

Then we're the easy conversation. SOC 2, GDPR, HIPAA, and ISO 27001 attested (reports under NDA), 32 compliance framework packs with exportable evidence, declared-region data residency with cross-region denial, RBAC, spend caps, full audit trail, PII shield enforced before you're billed, and an agent firewall that grades tool and MCP calls before they run. Self-host or private deployment if your policy requires it.

"Switching costs / lock-in"

Swap base_url to api.orcarouter.ai/v1 and you're live in 60 seconds, no credit card. It's an OpenAI-compatible surface, so your SDKs don't change. And OrcaRouter-Lite is open source with the same API surface: if you ever want to run it yourself, the escape hatch is on GitHub. Lock-in is structurally moot.

The real fee stack (third-party itemized analysis, May 2026)

Read this first. Their claim "no markup on provider pricing" is technically true per token. The money is extracted elsewhere, and this is the analysis that shows where.
  • 5.5% platform fee on credit-card top-ups, with a $0.80 minimum. Small top-ups effectively pay 10-20%.
  • 5% BYOK fee on every request above 1M/month.
  • Currency conversion friction on Stripe (EUR to USD) before the platform even sees the charge.
  • Net effect at $10k/mo: roughly $550+/mo invisible on any per-token chart. Your 0% markup story lands right here.

Where they win

  • Mindshare and community. The default "one key for everything" for developers
  • Catalogue breadth and day-0 availability of new models
  • Simple credit UX with no subscription commitment
  • Mature platform, enterprise tier, brand trust

Where you win

  • Total cost of ownership: 0% vs 5.5% + 5% fees
  • Learned routing (75.5% / 99%) vs a model picker
  • Governance: SOC 2, HIPAA, 32 packs, agent firewall, residency vs limited
  • BYOK economics: $0 fees vs 5% over 1M req/mo
  • Cache warmth: session-pinned vs pass-through

Talk tracks

If they come up early in the conversation

"OpenRouter built the category and built it well. They're an aggregator: per-token prices are passthrough, which is honest, but the economics sit in a 5.5% platform fee on credits and a 5% BYOK fee above a million requests a month. We removed the margin layer entirely and added learned routing on top. Same catalogue breadth, decision engine included, zero markup, zero BYOK fees."

If the prospect is already using them (displacement)

"You're paying for routing you're not getting. With OpenRouter you choose the model per call or set a static fallback, and the bill still carries the platform fee. Here's the honest part: don't take my word for it. Export your traffic, we replay it offline for a week, and you see what learned routing does to your spend with your own logs. Then decide."

If they get added late to the evaluation

Don't fight the brand. Reframe the evaluation from "which API has the most models" to "what does the total bill look like at scale, and who keeps your routing policy current." Offer the replay as the tiebreaker both sides can agree on.

Landmine questions (ask, don't accuse)

  • "What platform fee do you pay on credit purchases, and what's the minimum charge on small top-ups?"
  • "If you attach your own keys, what happens to your bill above 1M requests a month?"
  • "Who in your stack decides which model handles each prompt, and how is that decision kept current as prices change weekly?"
  • "What does the full cost look like including the fee layer, not just per-token price?"

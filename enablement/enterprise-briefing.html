Claim hygiene, before anything else

Every figure in this deck is vendor-reported: the 99%, the 40+%, the 75.5%, the 100% uptime. They are all true as published, and none of them are the pitch. The pitch is the 30-day replay pilot, which turns a vendor claim into a customer measurement. When a number gets challenged, do not argue the number. Open the replay.

Core line, learn it cold: "the only thing we have to sell you is a smaller bill."

89% of traffic is not hard, and 100% of it bills at the hard rate. The gap between the two bars on this slide is the money adaptive routing recovers, and the pilot measures your gap, not our illustration.

Say it like this

"The gap between those two bars is the money adaptive routing recovers. And the three costs of doing nothing: staleness, because frontier models ship weekly and nobody can re-benchmark by hand; governance, because teams adopt models faster than security reviews them; and all three are decided at the moment a request is assigned to a model, which is exactly where a router sits."

03

The method: a learned policy, not if-statements

Platform · ML Engineering

A policy that learns from every request, with offline rehearsal before any change touches live traffic. This slide is where you earn the engineers.

Say it like this

"The difference between us and an OSS gateway is this: it is a learned policy, not a cost table with if-statements. Signals come off the request itself: length, reasoning cues, code and maths density, tools. The policy picks a pool: economy for routine, balanced for mixed, frontier for hard."

"Two learning loops. Online: every completed request writes quality-against-cost back into the policy, so it is different tomorrow because of what it served today. Offline: replay re-runs your logged traffic through a fresh policy, A/B testing a change against your own history before it touches live requests."

"And you keep the controls: the objective per router, explicit bypass, pin any workload, off without a deploy. The whole decision costs microseconds of CPU, no GPU, no added model call."

Numbers to quote

0 model calls0 GPUmicroseconds CPU
What not to sayDo not say "it learns like GPT" or anything suggesting a model in the request path. There is no added model call and no GPU. The learning is in the policy, the decision is CPU work.
Likely question

What if it routes hard work to an economy model? Escalation promotes the whole session when a task outgrows its pool, reversibly, and the original channel pin is preserved so de-escalation and long-gap resumption return to the same model.

Likely question

Can we force a specific model? Yes, two ways: an explicit model name bypasses routing entirely, and any workload can be pinned. The controls exist so the policy is a recommendation with a leash, not a black box.

Appendix: how the router decides

ML Engineering

Four stages, no extra model call. This is the depth slide for when an engineer wants the mechanics, not the story.

Say it like this

"Four stages. Signal extraction reads structural and semantic signals straight off the request: prompt length, reasoning cues, system-prompt weight, code and maths density, tool presence, declared output budget. Conditional preference: the policy holds a separate preference per model, per output-budget band and per task class, so a model good at long-form reasoning is not automatically picked for a short tool call. Pool gating: a difficulty score narrows the candidates, so routine traffic never reaches a frontier model and hard traffic never lands on an economy one. Session escalation: sessions stick within a tier, and on threshold the session moves up with its channel pin following."

"Commercially, offline replay converts a vendor claim into a customer measurement. The harness reconstructs each historical request and drives the policy through your real traffic in order, so the number in the business case is yours, produced before any integration is committed."

Numbers to quote

4 stages0 extra calls30 days session continuity
What not to sayDo not imply the difficulty score is an LLM judge. Signals are read off the request; there is no auxiliary model call and no GPU in the path.
Likely question

One global ranking or per-task? Not one global ranking. The preference is conditioned on output budget and task class, so the router learns that a reasoning specialist is not the right pick for a short tool call.

The grilling

Q&A playbook

The objections that come up every time, with the answer that ends the argument. Note how many end the same way: open the replay.

Is the 75.5% vendor-reported?
Yes, and we say it first. The board is a floor; the replay produces their number on their traffic, and the threshold is theirs to set.
Why not just use an OSS gateway like LiteLLM?
A static rule engine with no routing policy, and you operate it. The comparison row says it: the routing policy it does not have is the whole product.
Aggregators give us discounts.
A 5–20% margin is an incentive that runs against your bill. We take zero spread, so the only thing we have to sell you is a smaller bill.
What about latency?
Under 50ms p50 and p99, no GPU, no added model call, decision in microseconds of CPU. We state it before they measure it.
We have multi-year contracts with OpenAI and Anthropic.
BYOK: attach your own keys, negotiated rates, commitments and data terms unchanged, and committed spend still draws down. Fee specifics are on the pricing page.
Where does our data go?
Region-stamped evidence with cross-region download denial, plus honest disclosure that upstream providers process in their own regions. The honest answer is the one that survives diligence.
What if we want out?
The Lite gateway runs the same API surface, open source, forever. The escape hatch is by construction, which is also why lock-in fear is not a valid objection.
How is this actually priced?
Subscriptions: free tier, $49 a month Team, volume-based Enterprise. Zero markup on tokens. The Enterprise commitment is a floor on spend routed through us, never a fee on top of your bill.
Can't we just pin models ourselves?
You can, and the controls let you. But the moment you pin everything, the 62% pays the hard rate again. Pinning is the override, not the default.
What if the router is wrong?
Escalation catches it, reversibly, and the offline loop learns from it. The measured cost of the saving is under 1% quality degradation, and the replay verifies that on their workload too.

Pricing semantics, the deal-winning rule

The Enterprise commitment is a floor on spend routed through the platform, never an extra fee. A fee sits on top of the bill and eats the savings math: a $5k fee against $10k of monthly spend destroys the 30–40% saving. A floor is how the saving gets shared: the customer commits a slice of the spend they are already routing, they keep the discount, and the revenue model stays subscriptions plus volume, never a token margin.

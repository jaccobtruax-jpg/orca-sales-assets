What we sell

One sentence, then the proof that follows it everywhere.

OrcaRouter is an OpenAI-compatible API gateway. Change the base URL to https://api.orcarouter.ai/v1, keep your existing SDK, and your requests route across the providers you already use at provider list price.

The commercial point is not a feature list. We do not take a cut of token spend. Our revenue comes from optional team subscriptions, which means the token bill is pass-through by construction. That single fact answers most of the questions a technical buyer will ask in the first call.

Claim hygiene, read this before you quote anything

Two inconsistencies are live right now. Marketing copy says 40+ providers while the docs models page lists 11 upstream providers, so say "200+ models across 11 upstream providers" unless someone confirms the wider count. And every leaderboard rank is a snapshot from a moving board, so check the live board and quote it with its date. Four pillars, in the order buyers care about them

This is the product's own framing: Route, Observe, Manage, Govern. Keep the language close to it, because it is what the site and docs already say.

Route

Grade each request, then send it to the model that answers it best.

  • Adaptive routing. Every prompt is graded and matched to a model. Online learning means the router keeps updating from production outcomes rather than a frozen benchmark run.
  • Model fallbacks. Chain up to five models. It fails over on upstream errors, and billing follows the model that actually served the request.
  • Named routers. Save a strategy and call it by name. Built-in strategies cover cheapest, quality, balanced and adaptive; a YAML and CEL routing layer handles the rest.
  • Auto router. orcarouter/auto exists the moment an account does and sizes the model to the request.
  • Session affinity. One header keeps a conversation on the same model so the prompt cache stays warm.
  • Frontier escalation. When a session outgrows the model it started on, that one session moves up. Named router traffic only.
Say it like this"One endpoint, every model. The router grades each request and sends it where it belongs, and if a provider wobbles the fallback chain catches it before your users notice."

Observe

Show the work. Every call carries its own receipt.

  • Request logs. Grade, model, latency and cost on every call, with copy-as-cURL.
  • Per-request cost. Read the exact billed cost inline on the response, or look it up afterwards by request id.
  • Cache arithmetic. Cached tokens and the provider's own unit price appear on the receipt, so the saving is auditable instead of asserted.
  • Insights. Routing, latency, spend and model quality in one view.
  • OrcaReplay. Record an agent run, replay it, and re-ask a turn against a different model.
Say it like this"You get a receipt per call, not a monthly mystery. When finance asks what a feature costs, you answer from the logs."

Manage

Change behavior without shipping code.

  • Prompt registry. Workspace-scoped system messages. Edit once and every bound key picks up the new version on the next call.
  • Playground. Test models in the browser and battle two side by side.
  • Integrations. Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, LangChain, n8n, the Vercel AI SDK, and an MCP server.
Say it like this"Change the prompt in the gateway, not in your deploy pipeline. The binding lives with us, not in your application."

Govern

Stop things, don't just log them, and leave evidence behind.

  • Guardrails. A workspace content policy that screens input and output. Run observe mode first to see what would have blocked against real traffic.
  • Agent firewall. Governs what agents are allowed to do. Tool calls, MCP dispatch and outbound hosts are graded before they run, and a rule can allow, audit, deny, sanitize, hold for approval or cap cost.
  • Compliance packs. Promote policies from observe to enforce with an impact preview, then export evidence as a signed PDF or as raw CSV and JSON with a read-only link for reviewers.
  • Residency and retention. A region you declare and can prove, where a cross-region read is denied rather than served. Prompts and outputs are processed in memory and never written to disk.
  • Keys and budgets. Scoped keys with their own limits, seats, spend caps, roles and an audit trail.
Say it like this"Guardrails stop the content, the firewall stops the action, and both leave evidence you can hand to a reviewer."

Caching is where an effective rate drops below list. Cached and repeated prompt tokens bill at the provider's cache rate across the 5-minute and 1-hour windows, and we add nothing on top.

Free model ids end in -free. They carry the same weights as the paid model they shadow and cost nothing, but they run under their own rate limits, and the lowest tier applies a per-request prompt-size cap.

Internal only: the commit motion

Never recite this as public pricing. The shape is commit % = 100 − savings %. Promise a 40% cut and the commit is 60% of what they spend today, prepaid monthly as credit. It is a floor on spend that already flows through the platform, not a fee, and not a discount. Worked at mid-market scale: $100k a year of spend, a proven 40% cut, a $60k annual commit billed at roughly $5k a month, and their net bill lands at $60k. The pilot proves the number before anyone commits a dollar, and the credit is what funds the infrastructure that delivers the saving. Who you are talking to

Same product, five different opening lines. Lead with the thing that person is measured on.

FinOps and finance

They are measured on unit cost and attribution. Lead with the zero-markup structure and the per-call receipt, not with routing cleverness.

Ask: What does a request cost you today, and how long does it take to answer that?

Platform and infrastructure

They own uptime and integration pain. Lead with one endpoint, fallback chains, and the fact that routing overhead sits under a millisecond by default.

Ask: What happens today when a provider throttles you mid-sprint?

Security and compliance

They need evidence, not assurances. Lead with zero retention, declared region, scoped keys, and the compliance packs that export as signed evidence.

Ask: What does your reviewer need to see, and who signs it off?

AI product teams

They want to ship on the best model without redeploying. Lead with named routers, the prompt registry, and swapping models without an SDK change.

Ask: How often do you change models, and what does that cost you in engineering time?

Agent builders

They worry about what their agents can reach. Lead with the firewall: tool calls graded before they run, MCP servers gated, cost caps per call.

Ask: What is stopping your agent from doing something you would not approve of?

Inference platforms and gateways

They may want to add us as an upstream and earn traffic automatically on price and performance. Lead with the numbers that their ranker measures: pass-through pricing, cache behaviour, failover semantics, error transparency.

Ask: How does your scoring read our failovers and cache hits?

Objections you will actually get

The first four come from live buyer email. The rest come from the questions that follow them.

They sayYou say
Other gateways add 5 to 20% on tokens. What is your cut?Zero. The fee column reads $0.00 on every model in the catalog, and our revenue is the optional subscription. Ask them to compare fee columns, not headline model prices.
Can we get volume discounts, rebates, or pricing off list?No, and it is structural rather than a policy choice. There is no margin to negotiate on tokens. Caching is the one place the effective rate moves, and it moves at the provider's cache rate.
Higher rate limits or reserved capacity?Limits are set per workspace, not per model, and we do not publish per-model ceilings. A higher ceiling comes from a plan upgrade. We do not offer reserved capacity.
Is there a partner tier or program?Nothing published. Take a proposal internally rather than inventing terms on the call.
How do we know the savings are real?Run the replay. Their logs, their quality bar, their threshold. The measured delta belongs to them, and if we miss the threshold they walk and keep the measurement.
What happens to our prompts?Zero retention by default. Prompts and outputs are processed in memory and never written to disk. What we keep is request metadata for billing and abuse prevention, plus a truncated upstream error message when something fails.
Does routing add latency?Under a millisecond by default. Semantic embedding is opt-in, and if you enable it you pay for that embedding call.
Why not just self-host the open-source gateway?OrcaRouter Lite is MIT licensed and readable before you install it. What the hosted product adds is governance, evidence and support. Self-hosting is a real answer, so do not pretend otherwise.

Demo flow

Six acts, minute marked. Every objection ends by opening the pilot rather than a debate.

  1. MIN 0-5 · OPEN

    Set the frame in two sentences: one endpoint, provider price, receipts. Then stop talking and ask what they use today.

  2. MIN 5-12 · DISCOVER

    Their stack, their model mix, what breaks, what a request costs, who signs. Listen for the pain they volunteer rather than the one you planned for.

  3. MIN 12-20 · PRESENT

    Demo against their own pain. Swap a base URL live, show a fallback chain surviving a forced failure, then open a receipt and read the number out loud.

  4. MIN 20-27 · GOVERN

    Show guardrails in observe mode, then the firewall grading a tool call. This is the act that wins security reviewers who join late.

  5. MIN 27-34 · NUMBERS

    Walk the pricing model. Fee column at zero, cached reads at provider cache rate, plans that change features rather than token price. If a deal shape is in play, use the commit math and say plainly that the pilot proves it first.

  6. MIN 34-40 · CLOSE ON THE PILOT

    Four weeks. Week one is an offline replay of their logs. Weeks two and three run shadow with no traffic shift while quality is compared against their bar. Week four is a canary. Nothing is signed until the measurement lands.

Talk ratio and listening mechanics

Aim for them talking more than you. Loop what you heard back in their words, mirror the last few words before you answer, label the emotion you hear, and leave silence after a question instead of filling it. The rep who talks least in discovery usually wins the technical evaluation.

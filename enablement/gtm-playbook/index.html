GTM Playbook for Repsv3

Competitor-aware · technical buyers · social-first · benchmarked messaging — October 2026

The thesis: this category is won with specifics, not spray. Every winning message has a number, a named model, or a named buyer in it. Every losing message could be sent to anyone.
The seven rules
  1. The money number leads. 0% markup · 30–40%+ net savings · 200+ models. Engineers respect a number over an adjective.
  2. The wedge is OpenRouter's own FAQ. 5.5% fee on credit top-ups, 5% crypto, BYOK 5% above $25K/mo, credits expire in a year. Quote their docs, not us. "No markup on inference — however we do charge a fee when purchasing credits."
  3. The Stripe acquisition is the trigger. "Your neutral referee is now owned by the house accountant." Governance questions follow the money.
  4. Proof, not vibes. Benchmarks, docs, a test on their stack. Fear converts short-term; proof converts long-term.
  5. Never overclaim. One wrong number ends the evaluation. "I'd verify that" is a strength.
  6. Persistence is the advantage. 70% of senders quit after touch 1. Replies concentrate on touches 2–3. Run all 3 touches, every time.
  7. Track reply rate per template. Kill below 2%, scale above 8%. The category average is 3.4%; top decile runs 10.7%.

The six rules for technical buyers
  1. Competence before product. Speak models, tokens, latency, SLAs, failure modes. One sharp technical point beats three value props.
  2. Proof over claims. The demo isn't the pitch — the docs, GitHub, and the hands-on test are.
  3. Respect the evaluation. They WILL test it. Make the self-serve path excellent; sales supports, never pushes.
  4. Never overclaim. Technical buyers verify everything and pattern-match hype instantly.
  5. Answer build-vs-buy head-on. "Most teams spend 3–6 months building multi-model routing, streaming failover, and guardrails. Then they own re-benchmarking a moving catalogue forever. That's a headcount line, not infrastructure."
  6. Arm the champion. Give the technical buyer the ammo for their internal sell: comparison table, fee math, security posture, TCO one-pager.

The three-touch social sequence (per prospect — the floor, not the ceiling)
  1. Comment. A genuine thought on their post, referencing a specific point. No product.
  2. Engage again within ~a week — comment or a sharp public reply on a relevant thread.
  3. Warm DM. Reference both touches. One question. Under 90 words. Small ask.

The 2-second test: a technical buyer decides to read or delete in under two seconds. Buzzwords, "curious if you're looking…", blocks of text, fear + exclamation = delete. Their name, their number, their models, one question = read.
